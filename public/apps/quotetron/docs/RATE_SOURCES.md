# Rate Source Notes

The rate helper uses static planning ranges so the app can stay fast, private, and free of paid APIs.

## Sources Reviewed

- BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, Photographers, May 2023 and May 2024 table context: https://www.bls.gov/oes/tables.htm
- FreelanceRate.me Photographer Rates 2026: https://freelancerate.me/rates/photographer-rates
- SideStackers Photographer Rates 2026: https://www.sidestackers.com/blog/photographer-rates
- Sengi Photographer Rate Calculator 2026: https://sengi.co/calculator/photographer/
- Indeed freelance photographer salary snapshot, updated May 2026: https://www.indeed.com/career/freelance-photographer/salaries
- Housecall Pro 2026 handyman and house-cleaning pricing guides: https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/how-to-price-handyman-jobs/ and https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/house-cleaning-prices/
- 2026 freelance design, web, and social-media rate guides including SoloPricing, FreelanceRate.me, and Vortenza.

## How To Use The Ranges

The app treats public freelance pricing guides as client-price context and BLS-style wage data as employee-pay context. Client pricing should usually be higher than employee hourly wages because freelancers cover equipment, unpaid admin time, taxes, insurance, travel, software, marketing, and slow weeks.

Refresh these ranges before a major public launch or any annual update, especially for licensed trades and high-liability work.
